Remote job scams: 14 warning signs and how to verify an employer

Fake remote jobs are a growing problem. Learn the red flags, how to check a company is real, and what to do if you've been targeted.

By the Flex.Work editorial team · Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

Remote jobs attract scammers for a simple reason: the whole hiring process happens online, so it’s easy to fake. Scams range from fake check fraud to identity theft to “tasks” that require you to deposit money. The good news is that nearly all of them share the same warning signs.

The 14 red flags

In the job ad

  1. Pay far above the market for simple work. “Earn $45/hour for data entry, no experience” is a lure.
  2. Vague duties and no named team. Real ads describe the work, the tools and who you report to.
  3. A generic email domain. Recruiters from established companies rarely use free webmail addresses.
  4. Urgency. “Only 3 spots left — reply today” is a pressure tactic.

In the hiring process

  1. Chat-only interviews on messaging apps with no video call or phone call.
  2. An instant offer without a real interview or reference check.
  3. Requests for ID, bank details or tax numbers before a written offer. Legitimate employers collect these during onboarding, through a secure HR system, after you have signed.
  4. The “equipment check.” They send you a check to buy a laptop from “their vendor.” The check bounces after you’ve paid.
  5. Any upfront fee — for training, background checks, software or “starter kits.”
  6. Task scams where you rate products or “boost” apps, see a balance grow, then must deposit money (often crypto) to “unlock” it.
  7. Reshipping or payment processing jobs where you receive packages or money and forward them. These can make you part of a crime.

About the company

  1. No independent footprint: no company website beyond a single page, no employees on professional networks, no news coverage.
  2. A website registered very recently or that copies a real company’s branding with a slightly different domain.
  3. A recruiter who won’t share a phone number, office address or the name of the hiring manager.

How to verify an employer in ten minutes

  • Find the job on the company’s own careers page, reached by typing the company’s domain yourself — not through a link in the message.
  • Check that the recruiter’s email domain matches the company’s official domain exactly.
  • Search the company name plus “scam” or “reviews.”
  • Look up employees on professional networks and check they have real histories and connections.
  • Call the company’s main number from its official website and ask whether the role and recruiter are real.
  • Insist on a video interview with a named person.

Safe habits

  • Never pay to get a job.
  • Never deposit a check from an employer you haven’t met and buy anything with it.
  • Keep your national ID number, bank details and copies of documents until you have a signed offer from a verified company.
  • Use a separate email address for job hunting.
  • Trust the pressure test: real employers give you time to decide.

If you’ve been targeted

  1. Stop all contact and don’t send more money.
  2. Contact your bank immediately if you shared financial details or sent payment.
  3. Report it to your national consumer-protection or fraud-reporting agency and to the platform where you saw the ad.
  4. If you shared ID documents, follow your government’s identity-theft guidance and consider a credit freeze where available.
