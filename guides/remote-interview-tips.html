Remote interview tips: setup, answers and follow-up

How to look, sound and answer like a strong remote candidate — including the questions remote employers always ask.

By the Flex.Work editorial team · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

A video interview tests two things at once: whether you can do the job, and whether you can work well through a screen. Prepare for both.

Technical setup checklist

  • Do a test call on the exact platform the day before.
  • Put the camera at eye level and sit an arm’s length away.
  • Face a window or lamp; avoid a bright window behind you.
  • Use wired headphones or a headset to remove echo.
  • Close notifications and other apps. Plug in your laptop.
  • Have a phone number ready in case the connection drops.

Questions remote employers ask

“How do you stay productive without supervision?”

Describe a real system: how you plan the week, track priorities and report progress. Give an example of a project you delivered independently.

“How do you communicate with a distributed team?”

Talk about written updates, documentation, when you choose a call over a message, and how you handle time-zone gaps.

“Tell me about a misunderstanding at work and how you fixed it.”

Remote teams lose context easily. Show that you clarify early and write things down.

“What does your work setup look like?”

Briefly describe your space, internet reliability and backup plan.

Questions to ask them

  • How many days, if any, are expected in an office, and is that likely to change?
  • What are the core hours or overlap expectations?
  • How are decisions documented?
  • How do remote employees get promoted here?
  • Is equipment or a home-office stipend provided?

Follow-up

Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours. Mention one specific thing you discussed and restate why you fit. Clear writing here reinforces your remote credentials.
