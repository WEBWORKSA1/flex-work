Async communication: how flexible teams work without endless meetings

Practical rules for writing updates, making decisions in documents and choosing when a live call is actually needed.

By the Flex.Work editorial team · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Asynchronous (“async”) communication means people don’t need to be online at the same time to move work forward. It is what makes flexitime, time-zone spread and four-day weeks possible.

When to go async, when to go live

Use async forUse a live call for
Status updatesConflict or sensitive feedback
Proposals and reviewsComplex decisions with many trade-offs
Questions that can wait a few hoursBrainstorming from scratch
Demos and walkthroughs (recorded)Onboarding and relationship-building

Write messages that don’t need a reply to clarify

  • Put the ask and the deadline in the first line.
  • Give context, options and your recommendation.
  • Link to the relevant document rather than pasting fragments.
  • Say what happens if nobody replies: “If I don’t hear by Thursday I’ll go with option B.”

Decide in documents

For decisions that matter, write a one-page proposal: problem, options, recommendation and risks. Collect comments for a set period, then record the decision at the top. Future teammates can see what was decided and why.

Agree response-time norms

For example: urgent issues by phone; direct messages within four working hours; email within one working day. Norms remove the pressure to be always on.

Protect overlap time

Most distributed teams still need a few shared hours. Find yours with the time-zone overlap finder and use it only for things that need to be live.
